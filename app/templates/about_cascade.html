Attack Cascades

Understanding the Flow of Cyber Attacks Through Archetypes

What Is an Attack Cascade?

An attack cascade is a structured representation of how cyber attacks unfold across multiple stages. Rather than viewing security incidents as single events, the cascade model captures the sequence of tactics that threat actors employ to achieve their objectives—from initial access through to impact.

The key insight: Most successful attacks are not single exploits. They are chains of activities, each enabling the next, forming a cascade that can be interrupted at multiple chokepoints.

Built on MITRE ATT&CK

The cascade concept is grounded in the MITRE ATT&CK framework, the globally recognized knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques based on real-world observations. ATT&CK categorizes attacker behavior into:

  • Tactics — The tactical goal an adversary is trying to achieve (e.g., Initial Access, Persistence, Exfiltration)
  • Techniques — The methods used to accomplish those tactics
  • Sub-techniques — More specific variations of techniques

By mapping attack cascades to ATT&CK, we ensure that our analysis reflects documented, real-world adversary behavior—not theoretical scenarios.

The Cascade Archetype Approach

Traditional risk analysis often focuses on specific threats: "What if ransomware hits us?" or "Are we vulnerable to phishing?" The problem is that threat actors constantly evolve their tactics. When one technique is blocked, they adapt.

What Is an Archetype?

An attack cascade archetype is a pattern of attack that describes how a category of threats typically unfolds. Rather than modeling "ransomware" or "supply chain attacks" as isolated events, archetypes capture the underlying structure:

  1. The entry path — How attackers typically gain initial access
  2. The progression — How they move through the environment
  3. The chokepoints — Critical stages where defenses can interrupt the cascade
  4. The impact — The ultimate business consequence if the cascade completes

Why Archetypes Matter

The archetype approach provides tactic-agnostic resilience. When threat actors change their specific tools or techniques, the underlying cascade structure often remains similar:

  • Phishing may evolve from malicious attachments to credential harvesting links, but the Initial Access → Credential Compromise → Lateral Movement cascade remains relevant
  • Ransomware groups may switch encryption methods, but the Access → Persistence → Data Exfiltration → Encryption pattern persists
  • Supply chain attacks vary by vendor, but the Compromise Trusted Channel → Leverage Access → Target Downstream archetype endures

OpenImpactCascade's innovation: By assessing risk at the archetype level rather than chasing specific threats, your analysis remains valid even as adversaries evolve. The chokepoints you identify today will likely be the same chokepoints that matter tomorrow.

How Cascades Drive Assessment Questions

Each cascade archetype in OpenImpactCascade has been curated to identify the critical decision points that determine whether an attack succeeds or fails. When you select an archetype for your assessment, the system generates questions targeting:

  • Frequency estimates — How often does this type of cascade attempt occur in your industry/region?
  • Vulnerability at chokepoints — What controls do you have at each critical stage?
  • Impact if completed — What is the business consequence if the full cascade executes?
  • Control effectiveness — How likely are your defenses to interrupt the cascade at each stage?
